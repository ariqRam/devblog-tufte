---
title: The Indonesian Language Defines Time by Sunlight
date: September 15, 2026
---
In many languages, there is a clear word representing the time before noon (in English, *am*), and the time after noon (*pm*). This is similar in Japanese (*午後*) or Chinese (*下午*). However, in Indonesian, the closest word representing the time before noon is *pagi*, which translates to *morning*. The time after noon is closely represented by *siang*, which closely translates to *afternoon*[sn: it doesn't directly translates to afternoon]. But both *pagi* and *siang* are colloquially used differently to *am* and *pm*. For example, 11 am is not considered *pagi* anymore. Some Indonesians don't consider 10 am as *pagi*. Likewise, 2 pm is considered *siang*, but 4 pm is not *siang* anymore, it's *sore*. The distinction feels unnatural, but from my experience, the distinction comes from the heat or the sunlight intensity[sn: Loosely defined by me, not a scientific unit of measurement.].

Indonesia is entirely in the tropical region of the globe[mf: images/climate-regions.png | The world's climate regions]. This means that the sunlight hours are almost the same all year. This could be the reason why the words used to indicate time correlate to how hot it is outside (or how strong the sun is). *Pagi* means before the heat of the sun is at peak. *Siang* is when the sun is hottest, when you better find some shades. *Sore* is when the sun is still out, but it's past its hottest. *Petang* indicates the absence of sunlight, but it's not quite the darkest of night (similar to *evening*). Finally, *malam* is just *night*.
